# Chapter 5: Strange Things

The coffee tasted wrong.

Anna set down the mug Andrew had just made and frowned. Metallic. Copper and pennies on her tongue.

"Too strong?" Andrew called from the studio door.

"No, it's fine." She smiled and dumped it in the sink when he turned away. The carafe smelled clean. The filter was new. Probably nothing.

Except her tongue still tasted like a battery.

She kissed the kids goodbye, grabbed her bag, and headed downtown.

At the office, Denise intercepted her before the turnstiles. "Somebody left this on your desk early this morning. No badge log."

She handed over a small white box with a gold bow. Inside: a lemon tartlette, perfect, glossy. The note under it read, in a neat, unfamiliar hand: For the future SVP. Sweet things ahead.

Anna looked up. “Who?”

Denise shook her head. "Cameras are 'under maintenance' this morning, apparently."

How convenient. Anna snapped the lid shut. "Trash it."

Her office looked out on the skyline, all glass and morning light. Her name was on the door: *Anna Park, VP of Mergers & Acquisitions*. The building hummed with activity around her—deal teams moving through the halls, meetings starting, calls being made. She set down her bag and opened her laptop. An unsent email draft sat open on the screen, blinking like it owned the place.

Subject: Re: Confidential—Board Materials

The body was blank. The addressee line half-typed: tom.harr—

“Denise?” Anna called, calm. “Did you open my email?”

Denise appeared. “I don’t have your password.”

“I know.” Anna closed the draft and changed her password anyway. It was probably nothing—an auto-restore, a ghost of an earlier search. Still, a small cold line slid down her back.

At ten, she met with her team. Marcus walked through the pipeline; Rachel flagged a visual snag; two analysts argued about an assumption as if the model might cry. It was all normal, all clean. Then the junior project manager slid into a seat at the edge of the table, exactly on time, exactly put together.

Jessica Hall had been on Anna's team for only three months, and people already loved her. She was precise without being flashy about it. She remembered birthdays and sent thoughtful follow-ups that made clients feel valued. Her work was impeccable, and she had the kind of warm smile that looked good in every photo.

“Morning,” Jessica said. “I brought copies.” She distributed folders, perfect tabs, perfect margins. 

“Jessica, you’re on the Archer diligence calendar,” Anna said. “We’re compressing the timeline. I want a redline by Thursday.”

“Done,” Jessica said. “Also—your coffee.” She set a fresh cup by Anna’s hand without breaking eye contact with the slides. “Double espresso. One sugar.”

Anna didn’t drink it.

The meeting moved. Numbers. Risk. Timing. Jessica’s voice when she spoke was soft but confident. When it ended, people filed out in twos and threes. Jessica lingered.

“Anything else I can take off your plate?” she asked.

“You’ve got plenty,” Anna said. “Focus on Archer.” A beat. “Who gave you my coffee order?”

Jessica’s smile didn’t move. “Denise. I asked so I could be useful.”

Anna nodded. “Thank you.”

When Jessica left, Anna lifted the cup and brought it to her nose. It smelled normal. She set it down. Her tongue still remembered the metal.

At noon, she grabbed lunch at her desk. A smell hit her mid-afternoon before she even reached her office after a bathroom break. Sweet, rancid. The small fridge under her credenza—she opened it and recoiled. Her yogurt had exploded. The seal was punctured, the date stamped fresh. 

Denise gagged softly. "That wasn't like that this morning."

"Toss it."

"On it."

Anna washed her hands and sat. A calendar ping. An email from HR. She clicked through her inbox on autopilot, deleting, filing, responding. The afternoon was a blur of numbers and calls.

At three, she stepped out to grab water from the break room. Two analysts stood by the coffee machine, voices low but audible.

"—Whitmore guy? SVP of Private Equity?"

"Yeah, external hire. Word is he's got serious credentials. Columbia, Wharton MBA, built out the whole PE arm at—"

"Wait, Whitmore? Like the Whitmores?"

"That's the one. Old money. Connected. Tom apparently loves him."

Anna's hand stopped halfway to the water cooler. 

"When does he start?"

"Soon. References just cleared."

She filled her cup slowly, deliberately. Her face showed nothing. When she walked past them, they stopped talking and smiled. She smiled back.

In her office, she closed the door. Her heartbeat did something unfamiliar—stopped, then sprinted.

Jason Whitmore. SVP, Private Equity. References cleared. Starting soon.

Anna closed her eyes and saw a streetlamp and a kiss that felt like a scene. She opened them on skyline and glass and a life she had chosen. The past did not get to walk into her building in a suit and take what she had bled for.

She hit call on Claire before she could talk herself out of it. "You will not believe who's been referenced for SVP of PE," she said when Claire picked up.

"Who?"

"Jason Whitmore."

A beat. "Jesus Christ," Claire said. "Men never die. They rebrand."

Anna laughed, sharp. "Tell me I'm fine."

"You're fine," Claire said. "You've been fine for twenty years without him. You'll be fine through whatever this is."

"Yeah," Anna said. She wished she believed it.

At five, she packed her bag. She threw the lemon tart in the trash herself. Her laptop went into her tote—deals didn't stop at five. On impulse, she opened her desk drawer and set a sticky note across the seam where it met the frame. It was something her mother would do: a tiny trap.

If this is moved, I will know.

She logged out of her desktop, turned off the light, and caught her reflection in the dark glass. Her expression was composed, controlled. Good. 

On the way out, Jessica caught up to her at the elevator. “I’m stopping by a gallery opening tonight,” Jessica said lightly. “In the Village. A friend of a friend. Emerging artists. You and Andrew should come sometime. You’d love it.”

Anna smiled. "Send me the name."

“I will.” Jessica’s smile brightened, pleased. “Have a good night, Anna.”

In the lobby, Anna checked her phone. A new text from an unknown number was waiting.

*Congrats in advance.*

She stared at it. The lemon tart. The metallic coffee. The yogurt. Jessica's too-perfect helpfulness. And now Jason, walking back into her life twenty years later, right when she's up for SVP.

Maybe it was coincidence. Maybe she was being paranoid.

But Jason Whitmore was real. And he was coming.

She deleted the text, pushed through the glass doors, and headed home.
