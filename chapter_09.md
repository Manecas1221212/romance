# Chapter 8: The Arrival

The email arrived on a Monday with no warning.

**SUBJECT:** Welcome, Jason Whitmore—SVP, Private Equity

Anna was in a meeting when it pinged. She glanced at her phone under the table and watched the blood drain from her periphery.

Jason. Here. In her building.

She excused herself and walked to the bathroom on shaking legs. Locked herself in a stall. Read the announcement three times to make sure she hadn't misread the name.

*We are thrilled to announce that Jason Whitmore will be joining Henderson & Associates as Senior Vice President of Private Equity, effective immediately. Jason brings fifteen years of experience in growth capital, buyouts, and strategic advisory. He comes to us from Whitmore Capital, where he led over $2B in successful exits...*

Anna's hands were shaking. She put the phone down. Picked it up. Put it down again.

Twenty years. It had been twenty years since that last fight. Since the breakup she still didn't tell people the real reason for. Since she'd walked away and built a life that had nothing to do with him.

And now he was three floors up.

By the time she got back to her desk, her phone was full of messages.

Claire: *ARE YOU OKAY???*

Jane: *Call me.*

Denise, in person, hovering: "Did you see the announcement?"

"I saw it."

"Do you… know him?"

Anna's smile was composed, practiced. "We went to college together. It's been a long time."

Denise's expression said she knew there was more, but she didn't push.

At lunch, Jane met her at a restaurant with no windows and expensive wine list.

"Tell me you're fine," Jane said.

"I'm all right."

"You're lying."

Anna took a long sip of her wine. "It was twenty years ago. I'm married. I have kids. He's just another colleague."

"He's your ex who you never talk about." Jane leaned in. "Does Andrew know?"

"There's nothing to know."

"Anna."

"Jane, I'm fine. Really. It's ancient history."

Jane studied her with the same look she gave analysts who lied about their models. "If he tries anything—"

"He won't."

"If he does, you tell me. I don't care what the board thinks. He doesn't get to make you uncomfortable in your own building."

Anna felt a flicker of warmth. Jane was cold to most of the world, but not to her. "Thank you."

"Don't thank me. Just don't let him in your head."

Too late, Anna thought.

That afternoon, she saw him.

The elevator doors opened on fourteen, and there he was—older, sharper, expensive suit, same confident smile. He looked at her like no time had passed. Like they were still twenty-two and he still had the right to comment on what she wore.

"Anna," he said. One word. Her name in his mouth after two decades.

"Jason." She stepped into the elevator. Pressed fifteen. Watched the doors close too slowly.

"It's good to see you."

"Congratulations on the role."

"You too. VP of M&A. I heard you're brilliant at it."

The elevator climbed. She could feel the heat of him standing too close. Smell the cologne—different from what he used to wear, but the same kind of expensive.

"We should get coffee," he said. "Catch up."

"I'm very busy."

"I'm sure. But we're going to be working in the same building. Might as well be friendly."

The doors opened. She stepped out without looking back. "Good luck with the transition, Jason."

His voice followed her down the hall: "See you around, Anna."

She didn't breathe normally until she was back in her office with the door closed.

Andrew called at four. "How's your day?"

"All right." She couldn't tell him about Jason. Not now. Not with everything else fracturing between them.

"Sophie needs a ride home from Emma's. I have a studio thing."

"What studio thing?"

A pause. "A collector."

Jessica. He meant Jessica.

"I can't leave early," Anna said, sharper than she meant to. "It's a work day, Andrew."

"Right. Work." He hung up before she could reply.

Anna stared at the phone and thought about all the ways a life could break apart—fast, like shattering glass, or slow, like erosion. She wasn't sure which was happening to her. Maybe both.

That night, she worked until nine. When she got home, the apartment was dark except for the studio. She could see Andrew through the glass, painting alone, music loud enough to keep the world out.

Jessica's cup was still on his workbench. The logo clear under the single desk lamp.

Anna went to bed without saying goodnight.

At two a.m., her phone buzzed.

Unknown number: *Some people don't change. Watch yourself.*

She called it. The line was disconnected.

She checked her email. Nothing.

She checked her calendar. A meeting had appeared for Wednesday: "Coffee with Jason Whitmore, 9 a.m., Starbucks lobby."

She didn't remember scheduling it.

She deleted it.

It reappeared.

She changed her password again. Cleared her cache. Restarted her phone.

The meeting stayed.

Anna sat in the dark, listening to Andrew's breathing in the other room, and thought about control. How much she'd always had. How quickly it was slipping away.

And Jason—back in her life like a ghost with a security badge.

She didn't sleep the rest of the night.
