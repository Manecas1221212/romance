# Chapter 26: Aftermath

Six months after Jane's conviction, Anna stood in her new office.

SVP. Corner suite. Floor-to-ceiling windows. Her name on the door in letters twice as large as before.

The view was better from here.

She'd earned it. Through blood and lies and survival. But she'd earned it.

"Congratulations." Andrew stood in the doorway. First time he'd visited her at work in over a year.

"Thank you. What are you doing here?"

"Signing the divorce papers. Lawyers said you wanted them notarized with witnesses. Professional setting."

Right. The divorce. They'd filed three months ago. Uncontested. Split custody. Equal assets. Remarkably civilized for two people who'd destroyed each other.

"Come in."

The lawyers arrived. Papers were signed. Witnessed. Notarized.

Just like that, seventeen years erased.

After the lawyers left, Andrew lingered.

"The kids want to know if you're coming to Jake's game Saturday."

"I'll be there."

"You've said that before."

"I'll be there," Anna repeated.

Andrew looked around her office. At the awards on the walls. The deals framed and mounted. The evidence of her success.

"Was it worth it?" he asked.

"Was what worth it?"

"Everything you sacrificed. Everything you became. Was the view from here worth the cost?"

Anna thought about Jessica's face. About the moment the life left her eyes. About the nights she woke up remembering and felt nothing.

"Yes," she said.

Andrew nodded slowly. "That's what scares me."

"Why?"

"Because I don't think you're lying. I think you actually believe that."

He left.

Anna stood at the window and looked at the city. Millions of lives. Millions of ambitions. All those people climbing, fighting, destroying each other for pieces of power.

She'd won. She was winning.

And Andrew was right—it had been worth it.

---

Jake's game was Saturday at ten. Anna arrived at 9:45.

Andrew was already there. Sitting with a woman Anna didn't recognize. Blonde. Pretty. Laughing at something Andrew said.

Sophie saw Anna first. "Mom! You came!"

"I said I would."

"You also said you'd come to my recital. And Jake's tournament. And parent-teacher conferences."

"I'm here now."

Sophie's expression was complicated. Hurt. Resignation. The look of a child who'd stopped expecting her parent to show up.

"Yeah. You are."

The game started. Jake played well. Anna watched. Cheered appropriately. Took photos.

Andrew's new friend—girlfriend? date?—leaned into him. He smiled. Actually smiled.

After, they all went for ice cream. The woman's name was Catherine. She was a gallery owner. Interesting. Kind. Good with the kids.

Everything Anna wasn't.

"Your kids are wonderful," Catherine said to Anna. Genuine. No threat. Just a statement.

"Thank you. Andrew's done a good job with them."

"You both have."

Anna wanted to believe that. But Sophie's careful distance and Jake's formal politeness said otherwise.

They knew. Not about Jessica—but about what kind of person their mother was. Cold. Absent. Capable of terrible things.

---

Monday, Tom called Anna into his office.

"The board wants to have dinner with you. Get to know you. Evaluate fit."

"For?"

"The open board seat. You're a frontrunner."

"Jane's seat."

"It's not Jane's seat anymore. It's an opportunity."

Anna thought about Jane's last words. *Welcome to the board.*

"I'd be honored."

---

The dinner was Thursday. Five board members. Anna in a designer suit that cost more than her first car.

They asked questions. She answered perfectly. Demonstrated vision. Strategic thinking. The kind of ruthlessness they needed in a board member but dressed up as "decisive leadership."

One member—Gerald, sixty-something, silver hair—leaned forward.

"I'll be direct, Anna. There are concerns. The situation with Jane van Moore. The tragedy with your employee. Some wonder if you're too close to controversy."

Anna held his gaze. "Controversy found me. I didn't seek it. But I survived it. And I kept delivering results. That's what leadership is—performing under pressure."

"Some might say the pressure broke you."

"Did it? I'm sitting here. SVP. Top performer. Stable. Focused. Whatever broke, it wasn't my ability to execute."

Another member—Patricia, fifties, sharp—smiled. "I like her."

Gerald wasn't convinced. "Jane van Moore mentored you for fifteen years. She orchestrated a campaign of psychological manipulation against you. How do we know you're not damaged by that?"

"Everyone's damaged by something," Anna said. "The question is whether the damage makes you stronger or breaks you. I'm still here. Still fighting. Still winning. You decide if that's strength or weakness."

After two hours, they thanked her and said they'd be in touch.

Anna went home. Poured wine. Sat in the dark.

Her phone buzzed. Claire. First time in months.

"I saw you're being considered for the board."

"News travels fast."

"Anna, are you okay? Really okay?"

"I'm fine."

"You killed someone. Jane went to prison. Your marriage ended. The kids barely know you. You're not fine."

"I'm successful. There's a difference."

"Is there? Because from where I'm sitting, you've lost everything that matters and gained everything that doesn't."

"The board matters."

"Does it? When you're sixty and alone in some expensive apartment, will it have mattered?"

Anna thought about Jane's empty penthouse. About the loneliness that had driven her to destroy what she couldn't have.

"I don't know," Anna said honestly.

"Then maybe figure it out before you end up like her."

Claire hung up.

Anna finished the wine. Went to bed.

Dreamed about Jessica. About choices made and lives taken. About the person she'd been and the person she'd become.

And when she woke up, she felt nothing.

Which told her everything she needed to know about who she was now.
