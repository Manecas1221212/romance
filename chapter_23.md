# Chapter 23: The Break-In

Anna waited three weeks. Watched Jane's patterns. Learned her schedule.

Jane went to the gym every Wednesday at 6 AM. Stayed exactly ninety minutes. Like clockwork.

Wednesday morning at 6:05, Anna entered Jane's building. Expensive lobby, doorman who barely looked up from his phone.

"Delivery for 18B," Anna said, holding an Amazon box she'd picked up from her own building.

He waved her through.

The elevator climbed. Anna's heart was steady. She'd come prepared—lock pick set (ordered online, delivered to a PO box), gloves, a story if anyone asked.

Eighteenth floor. Jane's door at the end of the hall. Anna checked the hallway. Empty.

Anna had practiced on her own door for three nights straight—same building developer, same overpriced brass hardware that was more about aesthetics than security. She'd watched YouTube tutorials until she could recite them in her sleep. Bought the tension wrench and pick set from a locksmith supply site that didn't ask questions.

Now she knelt at Jane's lock, the hallway silent except for the hum of the elevator shaft somewhere behind the walls. Her hands were steady. That surprised her.

Tension wrench in. Light pressure—just enough to feel the pins resist. Rake gently. Don't force it. Let the lock tell you what it needs.

One pin clicked into place. Then another.

Four minutes. Then—click.

The door opened.

Anna slipped inside. Closed it softly behind her.

Jane's apartment was immaculate. Modern furniture, expensive art, everything in its place. The home of someone who controlled every detail.

Anna started in the office. Desk drawers—locked. She picked those too. Inside: files, documents, a laptop.

She opened the laptop. Password protected. She tried a few obvious combinations. Jane's birthday. The firm's founding year. Nothing.

She moved on. Filing cabinet. More locks. More picking.

Inside: personnel files. Anna's file was thick. Notes dating back nineteen years.

*Hired 2007. Strong analytical skills. Needs guidance on political navigation.*

*2010: Promoted to Associate. Marriage to Andrew Park. Maternity leave 2013.*

*2013: Returned from first maternity leave. Productivity concerns. Work-life balance issues.*

*2018: Strong year. Recommended for senior manager.*

*2020: COVID adjustment difficulties. Personal life interfering with work focus.*

*2022: Recommended for VP. Conditional on demonstrated stability.*

*2023: Promoted to VP. Reputation cemented — hungry, reliable, unstoppable.*

Each note clinical. Observational. But also—controlling.

Jane had been documenting every aspect of Anna's life for nearly 20 years.

Anna kept digging.

Another file: emails. Printed copies of messages Anna had supposedly sent. Including ones she knew she hadn't written. The phrasing was close—close enough to fool IT, close enough to fool Tom—but wrong in ways only Anna would notice. Too formal in places she'd be casual. Too aggressive where she'd hedge.

Jane had been forging her voice.

Anna's hands went still.

At the bottom of the drawer, half-hidden under a stack of old board minutes, something caught the light. A USB drive. Small. Black. Unremarkable.

The label, in Jane's neat handwriting: "Backup Systems."

Anna stared at it.

Backup systems. The kind of label designed to be boring. The kind of thing no one would think twice about. IT backup. Server logs. Nothing interesting.

But Jane didn't keep boring things in locked drawers.

Anna pulled out the laptop she'd brought—her own, clean, untraceable. Her hands were steady but her pulse wasn't. She plugged in the USB.

No password prompt. No encryption warning. Jane had trusted the locked cabinet to be security enough. Nineteen years of arrogance, and this was the crack in the wall.

The drive opened.

Inside: software. Key-logging programs. Remote access tools. Scripts for sending emails from other accounts.

And a folder labeled "AP Project."

Anna opened it.

Inside: a timeline. Meticulously planned.

*Phase 1: Destabilize professional credibility. Small errors. Escalating frequency.*

*Phase 2: Introduce personal stressors. Recommend Jason Whitmore for PE role. Known history with AP. Will create emotional destabilization.*

*Phase 3: Exploit family vulnerabilities. Monitor husband. Encourage affair if possible.*

*Phase 4: If necessary, remove support structures. Isolate target from allies.*

Anna's vision blurred. She kept reading.

*Andrew Park showing signs of depression post-sister's death. Vulnerable. Potential affair target available (Jessica Hall, junior PM, recently single, history of boundary issues).*

Jessica. Jane had identified Jessica as a potential affair partner. Had... what? Encouraged it?

Anna scrolled further.

Found chat logs. Jane to Jessica:

*You should reach out to Andrew Park. His sister just died. He's a plastic artist. You love art. It's a natural connection.*

*Jessica: Is that weird? His wife is my boss.*

*Jane: It's compassionate. Anna works so hard she forgets to be present. Andrew's lonely—I've seen it. You'd actually be helping her marriage by giving him someone to talk to. She'll thank you for it one day. Trust me, I know her better than she knows herself.*

Jessica hadn't seduced Andrew. Jane had engineered it.

Anna kept reading. Found more.

Computer messages on a shady dark web forum. Transactions in cryptocurrency. A dealer contact. Fentanyl. Two months before Mia died.

A note in Jane's handwriting: *Mia Park. Addresses: three recent locations. Dealer contact: verified.*

Anna stopped breathing.

Scrolled to the next document.

*Mia Park overdose—Feb 14. Expected destabilization of Andrew Park. Resulting strain on marriage. AP will be distracted. Emotional. Vulnerable to manipulation.*

Jane had killed Mia.

Jane had bought the drugs. Found Mia. Provided enough fentanyl to guarantee a lethal dose.

Murdered Andrew's sister. To destabilize Anna.

But why? What was the motive?

Anna dug deeper into the folders. There had to be something. Jane didn't act without reason. Every move was calculated, every cruelty had a purpose.

She found it in a folder labeled "Board Correspondence."

Inside: screenshots. Email threads. Calendar invites. Private conversations between board members—conversations Jane wasn't part of. She must have accessed their accounts. Spied on them the same way she'd spied on Anna.

One thread stood out. David Chen, Patricia, Gerald. No Jane.

*David: Anna Park continues to exceed expectations. If the SVP transition goes well, I'd like to formally propose her for board consideration next fiscal year.*

*Patricia: Agreed. She's sharp. Polished. The kind of leadership we need going forward.*

*Gerald: And what about Jane?*

*David: Jane's become a liability. The rumors about her and the analysts aren't going away. If we're being honest, she's been protected too long because of her tenure. But tenure doesn't excuse behavior that makes us all look bad.*

*Patricia: If Anna performs, we have our succession plan. Jane steps down gracefully, or we don't renew her seat.*

*Gerald: She won't go quietly.*

*David: Then we don't give her a choice.*

Anna stared at the screen.

Jane had seen this. Jane had known. The board wasn't just considering Anna for SVP—they were grooming her to replace Jane entirely. And Jane, for all her power, was one scandal away from being pushed out.

Anna wasn't just a protégé anymore. She was a threat.

And Jane had decided to destroy her before she could take her seat.

Anna's hands shook. She took photos. Every document. Every file. Every piece of evidence.

Her phone buzzed. 7:20 AM. Jane would be back by 7:35.

Anna copied the files—just the files—to her phone and her laptop. Two backups. Two insurance policies. Then she closed the laptop, returned everything to its place.

Slipped out of the apartment. Down the elevator. Past the doorman who still didn't look up.

By 7:30, she was three blocks away. Walking fast. Then running.

She ran until she couldn't breathe. Until the horror caught up with her.

Jane had orchestrated everything. The sabotage. Jason's arrival. Mia's death. Jessica's affair with Andrew. All of it.

To destabilize Anna. To make her crack. To make her look unstable so Jane could keep her from the board.

Anna found a bench. Sat. Tried to breathe.

She had proof now. Real proof. Not conspiracy theories. Not paranoia.

Jane was a murderer. A manipulator. A psychopath.

And Anna was—

What was Anna?

A murderer too. Of an innocent woman Jane had used as a pawn.

The irony was almost funny.

Anna pulled out her phone. Called the one person who might believe her.

"Claire. I need you to listen. And I need you to believe me."

"Anna—"

"Jane killed Mia. She orchestrated everything. The sabotage, the affair, all of it. I have proof. I broke into her apartment and I have proof."

Silence on the other end of the line. The kind that meant disbelief—but Anna knew it would turn to belief soon enough. For now, the silence was enough. It meant someone else finally knew.
