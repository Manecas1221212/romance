# Chapter 16: Parallel Truths

**8:15 PM — Jessica's Apartment**

"Anna, what are you doing?"

Jessica backed toward the kitchen. Anna followed, gloved hands steady.

"Stop," Jessica said. "Just stop. We can talk about this."

"We are talking."

"You're wearing gloves. You're—Anna, this isn't you."

"How would you know what I am?"

Anna moved fast. Jessica stumbled backward, hit the counter. Reached for her phone.

Anna grabbed her wrist. Twisted. The phone clattered to the floor.

"Please," Jessica said. "Please don't—"

---

**8:15 PM — The Bar in Midtown**

Andrew sat in a dimly lit bar three blocks from the gallery, whiskey neat, contemplating how his life had fractured.

The gallery show had been good. Better than good. People had loved his work. For the first time in years, he'd felt seen.

And then Jessica had been there. Bright and warm and uncomplicated. And then the kids. And then—

"Andrew Park?"

Andrew looked up. A man stood there—expensive suit, confident smile, familiar in a way that itched.

"Yes?"

"Jason Whitmore. We met briefly at your show last night." He extended a hand. "Mind if I join you? I'd love to talk about your work."

Andrew hesitated, then gestured to the empty seat. "Sure."

Jason ordered a scotch. Settled in like they were old friends.

"Your pieces are fascinating," Jason said. "All those fractured faces. Women breaking apart. Very psychological."

"It's about multiplicity," Andrew said carefully. "How we contain different versions of ourselves."

"Or how some people wear masks until they can't maintain them anymore."

Something in Jason's tone made Andrew look up sharply. "Do we know each other?"

"Not directly. But we have someone in common. Anna Park."

Andrew's blood went cold. "You're the ex."

"I am. And you're the husband. Or the soon-to-be-ex-husband, I'm guessing."

---

**8:22 PM — Jessica's Apartment**

Jessica fought. Harder than Anna expected.

Nails raking skin. Elbows connecting with ribs. A knee aimed at Anna's stomach that Anna barely blocked.

But Anna was stronger. Fueled by rage and purpose and the kind of focus that came from having nothing left to lose.

She got Jessica on the ground. Wrapped duct tape around her wrists. Then her ankles. Efficient. Clinical.

"Anna, stop," Jessica was sobbing. "Stop, please. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry. Whatever you think I did—"

"You took everything from me." Anna's voice was calm. Flat. "My husband. My career. My stability. You wormed your way into my life and dismantled it piece by piece."

"I didn't! I swear I didn't. The affair was wrong, I know, but I didn't sabotage you. I would never—"

"Liar."

Anna pressed tape over Jessica's mouth. Silenced the protests, the pleas, the lies.

Then she stood and looked at what she'd done.

Jessica on the floor. Bound. Terrified. Still alive but not for long.

Anna felt... nothing.

No guilt. No horror. No hesitation.

Just the calm certainty that this was necessary.

---

**8:25 PM — The Bar**

"I should go," Andrew said, starting to stand.

"Wait." Jason's hand on his arm. "Just... give me five minutes. You need to hear this."

"I don't need to hear anything from you."

"You need to hear about who Anna really is."

Andrew sat back down, despite himself.

Jason took a long drink. "Twenty years ago, Anna and I were together. Columbia. Both finance majors. Both ambitious. I thought we were building something."

"This is ancient history."

"It's relevant history." Jason leaned in. "You know why we broke up? The real reason?"

Andrew said nothing.

"Everyone thinks I dumped her because she was too ambitious. Too focused on her career. That's the story she tells, right?"

"Something like that."

"It's a lie." Jason's voice was hard. "I left her because she was trying to control every aspect of my life. What classes I took. Who I talked to. Where I went. She'd check my phone, follow me to meetings, show up places I'd said I'd be alone. When I got an internship offer in London for the summer, she told me I had to turn it down. Said if I really loved her, I'd stay."

Andrew's stomach tightened.

"When I said I was going anyway, she... escalated. Started telling people I was cheating on her. That I was abusive. She sent my internship coordinator an email from my account—which she'd hacked—saying I was withdrawing. I almost lost the opportunity."

"That doesn't sound like—"

"It sounds exactly like Anna," Jason interrupted. "The woman who needs to control everything. Who sees people as pieces on a board. Who can't tolerate anyone having power she doesn't control."

Andrew thought about the GPS tracking. The phone monitoring. The way Anna had interrogated him about where he was, who he was with, what he was doing.

"The worst part," Jason continued, "was that she was convinced she was the victim. That I was the one hurting her by wanting independence. She couldn't see that her need for control was suffocating everything around her."

"Why are you telling me this?"

"Because Jessica called me this afternoon. Said Anna had been acting strange. Said she was scared."

Andrew went very still. "What did you tell her?"

"I told her to be careful. That Anna doesn't let go of things. That when Anna feels like she's losing control, she gets dangerous."

---

**8:32 PM — Jessica's Apartment**

Anna wrapped the tarp around Jessica's struggling form. The plastic crinkled, muffling the sounds.

Jessica's eyes were wide above the tape. Pleading. Animal panic.

Anna looked into those eyes and felt a flicker of something—not quite regret, but recognition.

This was the moment. The before and after. The line that couldn't be uncrossed.

If she stopped now, she could walk away. Untape Jessica. Claim a breakdown. Get help. Save herself.

Or she could finish what she started. Remove the threat. Reclaim control.

Anna thought about Andrew. About Jason appearing at the gallery. About Jessica introducing them. About the network they were building around her while she unraveled.

About how they all underestimated her.

She placed her hands on the tarp. Found the right position. Calculated pressure points and timing.

Jessica thrashed. The plastic tightened.

It took four minutes.

Anna counted each one, her breath steady, her hands unwavering.

By minute three, the thrashing slowed.

By minute four, it stopped.

Anna waited another sixty seconds. Checked for a pulse. Found none.

Then she stood up, peeled off the outer layer of gloves, and got to work.

---

**8:36 PM — The Bar**

"I need to call her," Andrew said, reaching for his phone.

"Who, Anna?"

"Jessica. I need to—she's with Anna right now. She texted me earlier. Said Anna wanted to talk."

Jason's face went white. "When?"

"Around eight. She said Anna was coming to her apartment."

"Jesus Christ." Jason stood. "We need to call the police."

"And say what? My wife is having a conversation with her employee?"

"Your wife is a control freak who sees Jessica as a threat. Andrew, I'm telling you—when Anna feels cornered, she doesn't retreat. She eliminates."

"That's insane. Anna wouldn't—she's not violent. She's just... intense."

"Intense is how psychopaths survive in corporate America." Jason was already dialing. "Where does Jessica live?"

Andrew stared at him. At his phone. At the terrible possibility crystallizing in his mind.

All the tracking. The monitoring. The cold, calculating way Anna had documented everything. The way she'd asked about Jessica with too much control, like she was cataloging evidence for a case.

"Perry Street," Andrew whispered. "128 Perry Street, apartment 4C."

Jason relayed the address to 911. "We need a wellness check. Possible domestic disturbance. Possible violence."

Andrew grabbed his coat. "I'm going there."

"We both are. But Andrew—if I'm right, if we're too late—you need to be prepared for what we might find."

They ran.

---

**8:45 PM — Jessica's Apartment**

Anna worked methodically.

Cleaned the surfaces Jessica had touched. Wiped down the duct tape, the tarp, the gloves. Bagged everything in heavy-duty garbage bags—triple-layered, airtight.

Moved Jessica's body to the bedroom. Staged it to look like she was sleeping. Peaceful. No visible marks on her neck where Anna had pressed.

Checked the apartment for anything with Anna's DNA. Found a hair on Jessica's sweater. Bagged it.

Checked her timeline. 8:47. She was ahead of schedule.

Anna changed clothes. Rolled the hoodie and jeans into a bag. Put on the clean outfit she'd brought—business casual, forgettable.

Wiped down everything one more time.

Then she walked to the door. Checked the peephole. Hallway empty.

Opened the door. Stepped out. Closed it softly behind her.

Descended the stairs. Exited through the back entrance into the alley.

Walked three blocks to the rental car. Drove away.

As she merged into traffic, two police cars passed her going the opposite direction, sirens silent but lights flashing.

Anna checked her rearview mirror. Watched them turn onto Perry Street.

She kept driving.

By 9:15, she was in New Jersey, wiping down the rental car. By 9:45, she was in an Uber headed back to Midtown. By 10:20, she was in her own car, heading home.

By 11:00, she was in her shower, washing away the evening. Watching evidence circle the drain.

By midnight, she was in bed, staring at the ceiling, wondering if she felt anything at all.

She didn't.

And that told her everything she needed to know about who she really was.
