# Chapter 28: The Board

Three months later, Anna got the call.

"Congratulations, Anna. The board voted unanimously. You're in."

She was the youngest board member in Henderson & Associates' seventy-year history. Forty-two years old. Power she'd dreamed of since Columbia.

The press release went out. *Anna Park Appointed to Henderson & Associates Board. First Woman Under 45 to Achieve Role.*

The articles called her a trailblazer. A survivor. An inspiration.

If they only knew.

---

The first board meeting was surreal. Anna sat in Jane's old seat. Same chair. Same view of the table.

She could almost feel Jane's presence. Watching. Approving.

"Welcome, Anna," said David Chen, board chair. "We're thrilled to have you."

The meeting covered quarterly numbers. Strategic planning. M&A pipeline. Anna contributed. Showed she belonged.

After, David pulled her aside.

"You're aware of Jane's... situation."

"Of course."

"The board wants to ensure we've moved past that chapter. That Henderson's reputation is intact."

"I understand."

"Good. Because we're counting on you to represent a new generation of leadership. Ethical. Transparent. Clean."

Anna smiled. "I won't let you down."

She meant it. She'd learned from Jane's mistakes. The biggest one: getting caught.

---

Andrew remarried a year later. Catherine, the gallery owner. A small ceremony. Sophie and Jake as witnesses.

Anna sent a gift. A painting—expensive, tasteful. No message needed.

Catherine sent a thank-you note. Handwritten. Gracious.

Anna filed it away and felt nothing.

Sophie was fifteen now. Starting to think about colleges. Wanted to study medicine. As far from her mother's world as possible.

"Do you hate me?" Anna asked once, over a lunch Sophie had agreed to out of obligation.

Sophie considered the question seriously. "No. I'm sad for you."

"Why?"

"Because you have everything you wanted and you're still empty."

The insight was sharper than any accusation.

---

Jake was nine. Looked like Andrew. Had Andrew's quiet kindness. He visited Anna every other weekend because the custody agreement said he had to.

They'd started running out of things to talk about.

Now they sat in expensive restaurants and made small talk about school, about friends, about nothing that mattered.

"Do you ever think about her?" Jake asked one Sunday.

"Who?"

"The woman who died. Jessica."

Anna's blood went cold. "Sometimes. Why?"

"Dad said she was nice. That she made him happy for a while. Before."

Before she died. Before Anna killed her.

"I'm sorry she's gone," Anna said carefully.

"Me too. Dad cries about her sometimes. When he thinks no one's listening."

Anna absorbed that. Andrew still grieved Jessica. Still mourned the woman Anna had murdered.

"Your father loved her?"

"I think so. Not like he loved you. But yeah."

Anna paid the check. Drove Jake back to Andrew's house. Watched him run inside without looking back.

She sat in the car and thought about how many lives she'd destroyed in service of her ambition.

Jessica. Dead.

Andrew. Broken.

Sophie and Jake. Damaged.

Jane. Imprisoned.

Claire. Distant.

And Anna? Sitting on a board. Commanding a salary. Owning power.

Hollow. Empty. Alone.

She drove back to her penthouse. Poured expensive wine. Stood at the window overlooking the city.

All those lights. All those lives. All those people who'd never know her name, her power, her sins.

Her phone buzzed. A message from an unknown number.

*Congratulations on the board. I always knew you'd make it. —J*

Jane. From prison. Somehow still reaching her.

Anna didn't reply. But she didn't delete it either.

Because part of her—the darkest part—wanted Jane to know she'd won.

Wanted Jane to see what her mentorship had created.

A woman who'd sacrificed everything human to become powerful.

A woman who looked in the mirror and saw a stranger.

A woman who'd gotten exactly what she wanted and lost everything that mattered.

Anna finished the wine. Went to bed.

And dreamed, as always, of Jessica's face.

Not with guilt. Not quite.

Just with the cold recognition that some prices, once paid, could never be refunded.
